THE NEW PRACTICAL SCIENCE AREA

Physics in baking.

Baking is a laboratory hiding in the kitchen: heat transfer, gas production, pressure, phase changes, evaporation, diffusion, viscosity and structural changes all appear in one loaf or bun.

EXPERIMENT 01 / YEAST

Why does dough rise?

Yeast ferments sugars and produces carbon dioxide. The gas is trapped by the dough's gluten structure, increasing volume. Change temperature or fermentation time and measure the effect instead of guessing.

See the experiment ↗
EXPERIMENT 02 / HEAT

How does an oven cook?

Heat moves from the oven environment into the dough while moisture moves outward and evaporates. Compare oven position, temperature and baking time.

Study baking science ↗
EXPERIMENT 03 / STRUCTURE

Why are some buns soft?

Investigate flour, water, gluten development, fat, sugar and gas cells. Make two controlled batches and compare mass, volume, texture and moisture.

Design an experiment ↗
EXPERIMENT 04 / MEASUREMENT

Turn a recipe into data

Record ingredient mass, dough temperature, proofing time, dough mass, baked mass, volume and cost. Then calculate yield, moisture loss and cost per bun.

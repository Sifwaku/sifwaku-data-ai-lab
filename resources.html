YOUR FIRST COMPETITION

Go from curious to your first submission.

A simple route through Kaggle: learn the workspace, understand the problem, make a baseline, and explain what you changed.

01 / PICK A START

Choose a beginner challenge

Start with Kaggle's Getting Started competitions. Titanic is a classic first project with starter notebooks and a clear submission format.

Browse beginner competitions ↗
02 / READ FIRST

Understand the rules and score

Before coding, check the problem, evaluation metric, timeline, submission limits and team rules. Your score is only meaningful if your validation method matches the task.

Read Kaggle's competition guide ↗
03 / LEARN BY EXAMPLE

Open a starter notebook

Read a public notebook, run it, and make your own copy. Change one idea at a time and keep notes so you can explain what helped.

Explore Titanic notebooks ↗
04 / FINISH THE LOOP

Submit, record, improve

Create a valid baseline submission, record your result, then test one improvement. Publish a short write-up with your question, method, result and next step.

Open the Titanic starter challenge ↗

FIND YOUR PEOPLE

Good teams make learning less lonely.

You do not need to be an expert to team up. Agree on one competition, a realistic time commitment and how you will share work.

01 / MEET

Ask in the competition forum

Say which challenge you chose, what you want to learn, what you can contribute, your weekly availability and time zone. Invite beginners and experienced learners alike.

Visit a beginner competition forum ↗
02 / MIX SKILLS

Choose complementary roles

One person can explore and clean the data, another can test models and validation, and another can keep the notebook reproducible and tell the story. Rotate roles as you learn.

Build skills together ↗
03 / JOIN ON KAGGLE

Set up your competition team

After joining a competition, use its Team page to invite members or merge teams where allowed. Check that competition's size limit, deadlines and rules before confirming.

Check current team rules ↗
04 / WORK WELL

Agree on a small routine

Set a shared goal, choose one place for code and notes, meet briefly each week, and make sure every member can run and explain the final notebook.

Learn GitHub collaboration ↗

A TEAM INVITATION YOU CAN ADAPT

“I’m joining [competition] to learn [goal]. I can contribute [skills] and spend [hours per week]. I’m looking for teammates who would like to [roles or goal]. I’m in [time zone]. Want to plan a first session?”
